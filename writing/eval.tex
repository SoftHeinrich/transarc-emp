\chapter{Distributional Inequality in the ARDoCo Benchmark}
\label{ch:inequality}

The standard evaluation of \sadcode traceability computes micro-averaged
precision, recall, and \fone on enrollment-expanded gold standards. This
chapter demonstrates that the structural properties of the benchmark---not the
system under evaluation---dominate this metric. We identify five compounding
sources of inequality that render file-level micro \fone misleading.

%% --------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Enrollment Inflation}
\label{sec:ineq:enrollment}

Before evaluation, gold standard entries annotated at directory granularity are
\emph{enrolled}---expanded to all individual files listed under that directory
in the project's code model (\texttt{.acm} file). \Cref{tab:enrollment} shows
that this converts 525~annotator decisions into 18,660~file-level data points
(35.5$\times$ average inflation).

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{Gold standard enrollment expansion for \sadcode.}
\label{tab:enrollment}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Project} & \textbf{Raw} & \textbf{Dir. Entries} & \textbf{Enrolled} & \textbf{Factor} \\
\midrule
MediaStore       & 57   & 3 (5\%)    & 59    & 1.0$\times$ \\
TeaStore         & 70   & 43 (61\%)  & 707   & 10.1$\times$ \\
Teammates        & 228  & 151 (66\%) & 8,097 & 35.5$\times$ \\
BBB              & 132  & 116 (88\%) & 1,529 & 11.6$\times$ \\
JabRef           & 38   & 38 (100\%) & 8,268 & 217.6$\times$ \\
\midrule
\textbf{Total}   & \textbf{525} &  & \textbf{18,660} & \textbf{35.5$\times$} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The inflation factor varies by two orders of magnitude: MediaStore (1.0$\times$,
mostly file-level annotations) to JabRef (217.6$\times$, 100\% directory
annotations). This creates two statistical distortions:

\textbf{Non-uniform weighting.} Each annotator decision carries weight
proportional to its directory's file count. JabRef's \texttt{logic/} expands to
972~file-level links---47\% of the entire enrolled gold standard. A single
correct or incorrect directory decision swings \fone by up to 4~percentage
points (\Cref{tab:sensitivity}).

\midrule
MediaStore  & 0.588 & 0.034 & 0.024 & 23/57 \\

\textbf{Illusory precision.} The metric appears computed over 18,660 data
points, but reflects only ${\sim}525$ independent decisions. The implied 95\%
confidence interval for JabRef (using enrolled count) is ${\pm}0.005$; the
actual CI (using raw decisions) is ${\pm}0.074$---15$\times$ wider.

\section{Block Correlation}
\label{sec:ineq:block}

Precision and recall assume independent observations. Enrollment creates
\emph{blocks} of correlated entries: all files under one directory share the
same TP/FN outcome because the system either identifies the component or it
does not.

We measure block homogeneity by checking, for each directory block of
${\geq}2$~files, whether all files are TPs or all are FNs
(\Cref{tab:homogeneity}).

\begin{table}[h]

the same TP/FN outcome (\transarc results).}
\label{tab:homogeneity}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Project} & \textbf{Blocks $\geq$2} & \textbf{All-TP} & \textbf{All-FN} & \textbf{Homogeneity} \\
\midrule
MediaStore  & 2   & 2   & 0  & 100\% \\
TeaStore    & 43  & 31  & 12 & 100\% \\
Teammates   & 149 & 130 & 19 & 100\% \\

Block homogeneity ranges from 96\% to 100\%. Files within a directory block
are not independent observations---they are redundant copies of a single

artifacts of directory enrollment.

\label{sec:ineq:amplification}

\section{Summary of Inequality}
\label{sec:ineq:summary}

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{Summary of distributional inequality sources in the benchmark.}
\label{tab:ineq_summary}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lp{6cm}c}
\toprule
\textbf{Source} & \textbf{Mechanism} & \textbf{Severity} \\
\midrule
Enrollment inflation & Directories expand to 1--972 files; one annotator decision becomes hundreds of data points & Critical \\
Block correlation & 96--100\% homogeneity; enrolled files are redundant copies of one decision & Critical \\
Amplification asymmetry & 1~SAD-SAM error $=$ 1--972 file FPs; file \fone weights errors by code size & Critical \\
Component-size skew & Top-3 AEs hold 38--99\% of all SAM-CODE links & High \\
Gold concentration & Top AE accounts for 45--48\% of SAD-CODE gold & High \\
Cross-project heterogeneity & SAD-CODE links/sent CV$=$1.734; projects are incommensurable & High \\
Ranking instability & 6/10 pairwise ranking disagreements across granularities & High \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

These biases do not merely add---they \emph{multiply}. A sentence links to a
popular model element (\sadsam long-tail), that element maps to hundreds of
files (\samcode concentration), and those files came from directory enrollment
(enrollment amplification). One correct sentence-component association thus
generates hundreds of file-level TPs. The file-level \fone is dominated by a
handful of high-impact decisions while appearing to be a fine-grained,
statistically robust evaluation.

\chapter{Comprehensive Evaluation Metrics for \sadcode Traceability}
\label{ch:eval}

Given the distributional inequalities identified in \Cref{ch:inequality}, we
define, compute, and assess a comprehensive suite of evaluation metrics for
\sadcode traceability. We organize them into seven categories: \fone variants
(\Cref{sec:eval:f1}), classification quality (\Cref{sec:eval:classification}),
enrollment-corrected metrics (\Cref{sec:eval:corrected}), sentence-centric
metrics (\Cref{sec:eval:sentence}), code-centric metrics
(\Cref{sec:eval:code}), bridge metrics (\Cref{sec:eval:bridge}), and practical
utility metrics (\Cref{sec:eval:utility}). All measurements use \transarc
results on the five ARDoCo benchmark projects.


%% --------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{\fone Variants}
\label{sec:eval:f1}

\subsection{Micro \fone (Standard)}

The standard metric: each enrolled \texttt{(sentence, file)} pair is one
equally-weighted data point.

\subsection{Macro \fone (Per-Sentence)}

Computes \fone per gold sentence and averages, giving equal weight to each
sentence regardless of its enrolled link count. A system that achieves high
micro \fone by concentrating quality on a few high-file-count sentences while
ignoring the rest will score poorly.

\subsection{Decision-Level \fone}

Each raw gold entry (directory or file, before enrollment) is one data point. A
raw entry is a TP if ${\geq}50$\% of its enrolled files appear in the result;
otherwise it is an FN. System results not matching any gold entry are FPs.

\subsection{Component-Level \fone}

Collapses file paths to their architectural component (via \samcode gold).
Each \texttt{(sentence, component)} pair is the evaluation unit. This evaluates
at the granularity where annotators actually made decisions.

\subsection{Inverse-Expansion-Weighted \fone}

Weights each enrolled link by $1/\text{block\_size}$, so all files from one
directory collectively count as~1.0. Every annotator decision contributes
equally regardless of directory size.

\subsection{Raw Entry-Level \fone}

Evaluates at the annotation granularity directly: each raw gold entry
(pre-enrollment) is matched against the system result. Strips away enrollment
entirely.

\section{Classification Quality Metrics}
\label{sec:eval:classification}

\subsection{Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC)}

MCC accounts for all four cells of the confusion matrix (TP, TN, FP, FN):
\[
\text{MCC} = \frac{\text{TP}\cdot\text{TN} - \text{FP}\cdot\text{FN}}
{\sqrt{(\text{TP}{+}\text{FP})(\text{TP}{+}\text{FN})(\text{TN}{+}\text{FP})(\text{TN}{+}\text{FN})}}
\]
Range: $[-1,+1]$. A random classifier always scores~0.

\subsection{Sentence-Level Exact Match Rate (EMR)}

For each gold sentence~$s$, check whether the predicted file set equals the
gold set:
\[
\text{EMR} = \frac{|\{s : \text{pred}(s) = \text{gold}(s)\}|}{|\text{gold sentences}|}
\]

Companion: \textbf{Mean Jaccard} $= \text{avg}_{s}\, |P(s) \cap G(s)| / |P(s) \cup G(s)|$
provides a softer measure of set overlap.

\subsection{Mean Average Precision (MAP)}

Ranks predicted components per sentence by confidence and rewards correct items
appearing early:
\[
\text{MAP} = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_s \text{AP}(s), \quad
\text{AP}(s) = \frac{1}{|G(s)|} \sum_{k:\, p_k \in G(s)} \text{precision@}k
\]

\Cref{tab:classification} shows measurements at \sadcode file level.

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{Classification quality metrics for \transarc (\sadcode file level).}
\label{tab:classification}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{MS} & \textbf{TS} & \textbf{TM} & \textbf{BBB} & \textbf{JR} & \textbf{Avg} \\
\midrule
MCC      & 0.633 & 0.828 & 0.814 & 0.820 & 0.917 & 0.802 \\
EMR      & 0.600 & 0.696 & 0.370 & 0.156 & 0.500 & 0.464 \\
Jaccard  & 0.613 & 0.696 & 0.493 & 0.675 & 0.918 & 0.679 \\
MAP      & 0.613 & 0.696 & 0.514 & 0.725 & 0.935 & 0.697 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

MCC closely tracks micro \fone (avg 0.802 vs.\ 0.803) because the evaluation
universe is extremely large (e.g., 159,984 pairs for Teammates) and true
negatives dominate. At the \sadsam level, where the universe is smaller
(78--2,772 pairs), MCC provides more discriminative value (avg 0.801 vs.\
\fone avg 0.799---similar overall, but with different per-project patterns).

EMR is the strictest metric: BBB's EMR=0.156 means only 15.6\% of sentences
have their file sets predicted exactly correctly, despite \fone=0.831. File-level
exact match requires both correct component classification \emph{and} perfect
\samcode, making it inherently hard.


%% --------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Enrollment-Corrected Metrics}
\label{sec:eval:corrected}

\subsection{Amplification-Corrected \fone (\acfone)}

Weights each file-level link by the inverse of its component's file count:
\[
w(s, f) = \frac{1}{|\text{files}(\text{component}(f))|}, \quad
\text{ACF}_1 = \fone\!\left(\textstyle\sum w{\cdot}\text{TP},\;
\sum w{\cdot}\text{FP},\; \sum w{\cdot}\text{FN}\right)
\]

Every component-level decision contributes equally regardless of code size.

\subsection{Normalized Discrimination Gain (NDG)}

Normalizes system \fone between random and oracle baselines, enabling cross-project
comparison:
\[
\text{NDG} = \frac{F_1^{\text{sys}} - F_1^{\text{random}}}
{F_1^{\text{oracle}} - F_1^{\text{random}}}
\]
Range: $[0,1]$. NDG=0 means no better than random; NDG=1 means oracle-level.

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{Enrollment-corrected and normalized metrics for \transarc.}
\label{tab:corrected}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{MS} & \textbf{TS} & \textbf{TM} & \textbf{BBB} & \textbf{JR} \\
\midrule
Micro \fone     & 0.588 & 0.829 & 0.821 & 0.831 & 0.943 \\
\acfone         & 0.739 & 0.851 & 0.624 & 0.347 & 0.857 \\
$\Delta$ (ACF\textsubscript{1}$-$\fone) & +0.151 & +0.022 & $-$0.197 & $-$0.484 & $-$0.086 \\
\midrule
Random \fone    & 0.148 & 0.120 & 0.055 & 0.059 & 0.335 \\
Oracle \fone    & 1.000 & 1.000 & 0.881 & 0.968 & 1.000 \\
NDG             & 0.516 & 0.806 & 0.927 & 0.850 & 0.915 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\acfone produces the largest corrections of any metric. BBB drops from
\fone=0.831 to \acfone=0.347---a 0.484 gap---because \transarc produces
139~FPs on small components that are invisible in standard \fone but dominate
when each component is weighted equally. MediaStore \emph{rises} from 0.588 to
0.739 because its annotations are mostly file-level with minimal inflation.

NDG reveals that MediaStore's \fone=0.588 corresponds to only NDG=0.516
(capturing 52\% of the achievable gap above random), while Teammates'
\fone=0.821 corresponds to NDG=0.927 (near the theoretical ceiling of 0.881).
The ceiling for Teammates is below 1.0 because 69\% of Teammates FNs are
structurally impossible---non-transitive gold links that no transitive
approach can recover.

\textbf{Coverage} $= |\{s \in \text{gold} : \exists\,\text{TP in } s\}| / |\text{gold sentences}|$.
The fraction of gold sentences receiving at least one correct link.

\textbf{Completeness} $= \text{avg}_{s:\text{covered}}\, |\text{TP}(s)| / |\text{gold}(s)|$.
Among covered sentences, the average fraction of gold links found. Near 1.0
across all projects (0.946--1.000), confirming the all-or-nothing pattern:
once a sentence is covered, almost all its links are recovered.

\textbf{Usefulness} $= |\{s \in \text{result} : \text{TP}(s) > \text{FP}(s)\}| / |\text{result sentences}|$.
The fraction of result sentences where correct links outnumber false ones---a
proxy for developer trust. Teammates drops to 0.692: for 31\% of returned
sentences, false positives outnumber true positives.

\textbf{Noise} $= \text{avg}_{s}\, \text{FP}(s) / (\text{TP}(s) + \text{FP}(s))$.
Per-sentence FP fraction. Teammates noise=0.299 means developers see $\sim$30\%
wrong files per query.

\textbf{All-or-Nothing Rate.} Fraction of gold sentences with recall either 0\%
or 100\%. Ranges from 84\% (BBB) to 100\% (TeaStore, JabRef). The system
either fully recovers a sentence's links or misses them entirely---a direct
consequence of block-level recovery from enrollment.

\textbf{Recall Gini.} Gini coefficient of per-sentence recall. Teammates'
Gini=0.405 indicates substantial inequality: quality is concentrated on a few
sentences while 40\% get zero recall.

\textbf{Worst-Q25 \fone.} \fone of the bottom quartile of sentences (by
per-sentence \fone). MediaStore, TeaStore, and Teammates all have
Worst-Q25=0.000, meaning the bottom 25\% of sentences get zero useful results.

\textbf{Harmonic Usefulness Score (HUS).}
\[
\text{HUS} = \frac{2 \cdot \text{Coverage} \cdot \text{Purity}}
{\text{Coverage} + \text{Purity}}, \quad
\text{Purity} = \frac{|\{s \in \text{result} : \text{FP}(s) = 0\}|}
{|\text{result sentences}|}
\]
The harmonic mean of coverage and purity, capturing the developer experience.
JabRef has HUS=0.667 despite \fone=0.943: coverage=1.000 but purity=0.500
(half of returned sentences contain FPs). BBB's HUS=0.405 reflects high
coverage (0.822) undermined by low purity (0.268).

Several observations emerge:

\textbf{High-value metrics correct specific biases.} \acfone corrects
enrollment inflation. Macro \fone corrects non-uniform sentence weighting.
NDG corrects cross-project incomparability. HUS corrects the disconnect
between aggregate \fone and per-sentence developer experience. These four
metrics, combined with coverage and noise, form a \emph{minimal sufficient
set} for comprehensive \sadcode evaluation.

\textbf{Some metrics lack discriminative power.} Completeness, completeness
gap, median precision, overwhelm ratio, and pollution are near their
extremes (1.0 or 0.0) for all projects, making them uninformative for
distinguishing systems. This is a consequence of the all-or-nothing
recovery pattern: once a sentence is covered, it is fully recovered with
high precision.

\textbf{Some metrics are redundant.} Decision-level \fone and weighted \fone
are nearly identical (avg difference 0.002). MCC closely tracks micro \fone at
the \sadcode level. These add complexity without insight.

\textbf{Some metrics address the wrong level.} MAP is meaningful only when
systems produce confidence scores, which most current TLR systems do not.
Component coverage evaluates \samcode quality, not end-to-end \sadcode.
Bridge metrics diagnose pipeline internals rather than output quality.

\section{Recommended Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval:protocol}

Based on this analysis, we recommend the following metrics for \sadcode
evaluation:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Micro \fone} (standard) for backward compatibility.
\item \textbf{\acfone} to reveal enrollment bias. When $|\fone - \acfone| >
  0.1$, the evaluation is dominated by a few large components.
\item \textbf{Macro \fone} (per-sentence) to reveal sentence-level inequality.
  When macro $\ll$ micro, quality is concentrated on a few sentences.
\item \textbf{Coverage} and \textbf{noise} for developer experience:
  what fraction of sentences get results, and what fraction of results are wrong.
\item \textbf{NDG} for cross-project comparison, normalizing by difficulty.
\item \textbf{HUS} as a single-number developer experience score.
\item \textbf{Raw decision count} and \textbf{enrollment factor} to
  contextualize the statistical reliability of micro \fone.
\end{enumerate}
